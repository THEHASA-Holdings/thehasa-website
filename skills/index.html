    We have been importing vehicles from Japan since 2025. You choose the car. We confirm it, buy it, ship it and have it inspected, then clear and register it with URA in Uganda, or with the DGDA and DGI in the Democratic Republic of Congo, and deliver it to you.

    Ugandan number plates

    We clear the vehicle with the Uganda Revenue Authority (URA), pay the import taxes on your behalf from your payment, register it with URA, and deliver it to you anywhere in Uganda.

    DRC number plates

    Ordering from the Democratic Republic of Congo? Your vehicle is shipped through Dar es Salaam and enters the DRC at the Goli border, crossing into Mahagi in Ituri Province. There it is declared and cleared with the DRC customs authority, the DGDA (Direction Générale des Douanes et Accises), and then registered with the DGI (Direction Générale des Impôts), which issues DRC number plates. Our partners in the DRC handle both, and we deliver the vehicle to you there.

    How vehicle import works

    1. Request. Send us the link to a car, or describe what you want.
    2. Quote. We send a written landed-cost quote: car price, shipping, inspection, import taxes (URA in Uganda or DGDA in the DRC), registration and our fee.
    3. Agree and pay a deposit. Work starts only after you accept the quote in writing.
    4. Purchase. The car is bought from the exporter in Japan.
    5. Inspection. A UNBS-approved inspection company checks the car before export.
    6. Shipping. For Uganda, through the port of Mombasa or Dar es Salaam. For the DRC, through Dar es Salaam. Then by road to you.
    7. Clearance and registration. For Ugandan plates, the Uganda Revenue Authority (URA) assesses the taxes, and the car is cleared and registered with URA. For DRC plates, the car is declared and cleared with the DGDA at the Goli and Mahagi border, and registered with the DGI, which issues the plates.
    8. Delivery. We hand over the car and all papers, in Uganda or in the DRC. Your written quote tells you the expected delivery time.
    Request a car

    Rules every buyer should know

    • Uganda does not allow vehicles 15 years old or older to be imported for registration on Ugandan plates. Our request form checks this for you.
    • Vehicles registered on DRC plates follow DRC rules, which we confirm with you before you order.
    • Used vehicles need a road worthiness inspection before export, under the UNBS pre-export verification system.
    • In Uganda, URA sets import taxes from the car's value, age, engine size and type. Older cars can attract an environmental levy.
    • In the DRC, import duties are assessed by the DGDA, and the DGI registers the vehicle and issues the plates.

    Rules checked November 2026. We recheck with URA and our DRC partners every quarter.

    THEHASA Real Estate and Construction

    Build with us

    Houses, schools, shops, offices, churches, warehouses or any other building. You deal with one registered company that is accountable for the whole project, from the first drawing to the day we hand you the keys.

    Building from abroad?

    Many of our clients live outside Uganda. You do not need to be here, and you never have to send money to a person.

    • Every payment goes only to the company account: Equity Bank Uganda Limited, account name THEHASA HOLDINGS COMPANY LIMITED. The account number is on your written contract and invoices.
    • You pay in stages, as each stage of the building is finished and shown to you.
    • You receive photos and videos every week, and a written report every month.
    • A family member or representative can visit the site at any time, and you can sign documents from abroad.

    What makes us accountable

    • A registered Ugandan company (Reg. No. 80020002846819, TIN 1017694800) that you can hold to a written contract.
    • A fixed scope, price and timeline, set out in a Bill of Quantities and a signed contract.
    • No change to the work or the price without your written approval.
    • Qualified engineers and a site supervisor on every project.
    • A defects period after handover, set in your contract, when we return to fix any fault at our cost.

    From your idea to your keys

    1. 1. Tell us what you want to build. Use the form below: type of building, location, whether you own the land, and whether you already have plans.
    2. 2. Consultation and site visit. We talk with you by phone or video, visit the site, and check the land title and boundaries with you before anything else.
    3. 3. Drawings. If you have no plans, our architect and engineer prepare the architectural and structural drawings with you, for your approval.
    4. 4. Approvals. We submit the plans to the local authority for approval and the building permit. Building starts only once these are granted.
    5. 5. Bill of Quantities and quotation. A Bill of Quantities lists every material and every task, with costs. You get a clear price, a timeline and a stage-by-stage payment schedule.
    6. 6. Contract. We sign a written construction contract covering the drawings, the Bill of Quantities, the price, the payment stages, the timeline, how changes are agreed, the defects period and how any dispute is settled.
    7. 7. First payment and mobilisation. You pay the first instalment into the company account. We issue a receipt, buy materials and set up the site.
    8. 8. Construction in stages. Typical stages are foundation, walls, roofing and finishes. Each stage is inspected and shown to you in photos and video before the next payment is due.
    9. 9. Reports and site visits. Weekly photo and video updates, a monthly report of progress and spending against the Bill of Quantities, and open site visits for you or your representative.
    10. 10. Changes, only in writing. If you want something changed, we give you the cost and time first. Work changes only after you approve in writing.
    11. 11. Final inspection. We walk through the finished building with you or your representative, list anything that needs fixing, and fix it.
    12. 12. Handover. We apply for the occupation permit from the local authority where it is required, and hand over the keys, the final drawings, all receipts and the completion certificate, and start the defects period.

    Training in action

    Building the capacity of our artisans

    As lead master artisan, we train the artisans who train the young people: work-based learning, continuous assessment, lesson planning and schemes of work, so every trainee reaches the same standard.

    Two ways to work with us

    Lead master artisan

    We train trainees ourselves, and also lead and supervise a network of local artisans in their own workshops. We build each artisan's capacity so every trainee reaches the same expected standard of competence, and all trainees are assessed through our UVTAB-accredited centre. Over the last two years we have led more than 30 artisans this way under the RETI Project.

    Centre-based master artisan

    Trainees are placed at THEHASA Vocational Skills Development Centre, a UVTAB-accredited centre (No. UVT692), and trained there by the centre's own trainers, with THEHASA as the master artisan responsible to you. The centre was first accredited by the Directorate of Industrial Training (DIT) and moved to UVTAB when Uganda's TVET law came into force.

    What you can count on

    • One accountable partner. We answer for delivery, quality and reporting across every artisan and trainee.
    • Assessment handled properly. We work closely with UVTAB and register trainees online through the UVTAB EMIS system, from application to invoice and payment.
    • A pathway to work. Because we also run construction, supply and other businesses, we can offer work to graduates in the trades they trained in.

    Who we work with

    NGOs, UN agencies, government programmes, universities and projects that need a private sector partner for youth skills training. Partners have included Bishop Stuart University (the Mastercard Foundation-funded RETI Project), Alight, ELECU, Planning for Tomorrow Youth Organization (P4T), Cheshire Services Uganda, AVSI and Plan International Uganda.

    4. Training. At our centre, or with our network of supervised artisans.
    5. Assessment. UVTAB assessment through our accredited centre, where your plan includes it.
    6. Reporting. Attendance, progress, assessment results and completion reports.

    Buyers never pay a deposit to an agent personally. Payments go through an advocate or to the company account under a written agreement.

    How we got here

    THEHASA stands for The Happy and Satisfied. Our founding directors share one belief: the happy and satisfied are those who depend on their skills.

    Young people from Kyangwali Refugee Settlement were being taken to Hoima for vocational training. The lessons were given in English or Runyoro-Runyakitara, which most Congolese learners did not speak, and many could not follow.

    Three people saw the gap: a hairdresser, a tailor and a social worker. In December 2020, during COVID-19, they incorporated THEHASA Holdings Company Limited to deliver training inside the settlement, in languages learners understand.

    The young people who needed training could not pay for it, so we moved the payer. As master artisan, the company contracted with partner organisations that paid per trainee, including Bishop Stuart University (the Mastercard Foundation-funded RETI Project), Alight, ELECU, Planning for Tomorrow Youth Organization (P4T), Cheshire Services Uganda, AVSI and Plan International Uganda.

    That model showed its limits. Revenue followed a few contracts whose timing others controlled, and no commercial contract pays for what young mothers needed most, such as childcare during training.

    So the founding directors split the work. The training centre moved to THEHASA Foundation, a separate non-profit that can take grants and spend all its income on its mission. THEHASA Holdings kept the trade, and commits 75% of its net profits to the Foundation.

    The company still works as master artisan. Organisations that need a private sector partner for youth skills training contract THEHASA, and we place their trainees at THEHASA Vocational Skills Development Centre, now run by THEHASA Foundation. Today the company trades through five businesses, with agribusiness as its growth line.

    THEHASA Foundation

    THEHASA Holdings Company Limited and THEHASA Foundation are separate organisations. The company runs the businesses on this site. The Foundation is a non-profit company limited by guarantee, led by women, with its own board and accounts. THEHASA Holdings co-founded it in 2024, as its corporate co-founder, with its Managing Director, Kabasinguzi Modestar, and handed it the training centre so it could grow as a non-profit. It decides its own programmes and how its money is spent. THEHASA Holdings, as its founding commercial partner, commits 75% of its net profits to the Foundation's programmes. Any dealings between the two are set out in written agreements, recorded in both sets of accounts, and governed by a conflict of interest policy. The two organisations have met to identify where their interests could conflict and agreed how each case is handled. Anyone with an interest declares it in writing and stays out of the decision, and declarations are signed regularly.
